Atlas / AI media

A reviewable workflow for research-led video

I built Atlas to carry source material through scripting and scene planning to a finished video. I can review the narration and revise individual scenes before rendering.

The story

Why I built it.

I built Atlas around the handoffs in a research-led video. A source informs the script, and the script gives each scene a job. I wanted to be able to revisit a decision after seeing the storyboard or hearing the narration. I gave each stage its own place in the project, with controls to regenerate selected clips and change their visuals. I tested the whole workflow on a video about a seed growing into a seedling and took the project through to export.

The problem

What it solves.

The script depends on the research, and the visuals have to fit the narration. A correction to one scene needs to carry through to the final video without forcing the author to redo the rest of the project.

Who it’s for: Creators making narrated explainers or documentary-style videos.

Workflow

How Atlas works.

  1. 01Develop the scriptBring in documents and links, then review the research and narration.
  2. 02Plan the scenesChoose footage or graphics and use the narration audio to set scene timing.
  3. 03Review and renderRevise selected scenes or clips, then assemble the video for export.

Engineering

Decisions and limits.

I use a Go service to coordinate stages and separate services for speech and rendering. Each project keeps its script, storyboard and media together. Checks can flag numbers in generated graphics when the permitted source text doesn’t support them.

I’ve used Atlas to finish an end-to-end test video. The source checks cover specific numeric inputs, and editorial review is still part of the workflow.
