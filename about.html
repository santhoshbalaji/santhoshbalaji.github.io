About

From production software to field work.

I’m Santhosh Balaji S, a software engineer and GIS consultant based in Chennai. I’ve worked on Zoho Meeting’s production media server and built GIS software for both commissioned work and my own products.

2017 to 2023

Compilers and a production media server.

I started at Zoho Schools of Learning in 2017 and spent 18 months as a full-time intern in Zoho engineering teams. In 2019 I joined Zoho as a Member of Technical Staff. I contributed to compiler and programming-language frameworks and worked on application platform components.

At Zoho Meeting, I investigated audio, video and screen-sharing problems in production, including when bandwidth was limited. I worked on recording layouts that followed the active speaker. The recording feature supported sessions with up to 50 video participants. I mentored five team members during my time at Zoho.

2024 to 2025

Learning the work from forest officers.

In February 2024 I joined a state forest department as a web developer. I sat with senior forest officers and departmental teams to understand work they were doing offline. I used what I learnt to design databases and build applications, and I refined the software with feedback from officers.

I modelled a computerisation programme that covered more than 20 modules. My implementation work included operational dashboards and workflow automation. Some of that work also involved maps, including applications for forest management and fire prevention.

The turning point

What the fire-prevention demo led to.

Forest-fire prevention was a case where officers needed to see operational information in its geographic context. I built a GIS prototype around that need and demonstrated it to officials.

The demo helped make the case for two commissioned geospatial projects. I took them on as an independent consultant after leaving my job in June 2025. I delivered one application end to end, from design through deployment. I’m still working on the second.

2026

Building my own GIS products.

The forestry work showed me how difficult GIS tools could be to use in an operational workflow. In May 2026 I started MapSmith, an enterprise GIS platform that keeps data publishing, map authoring and analysis in one workspace.

In July I started JurisField. I built it to address the same difficulty from the field, where people need their assignments and maps even when they’re offline. I built both products independently. JurisField’s first end-to-end version took me about a month.

How I work

I direct the agents and decide what ships.

I start with the people who will use the software. I prefer to show them a working prototype early and use their feedback to guide the build.

In my own development work, I direct coding agents such as Codex and Claude Code. Between 2 and 19 November 2026 I ran 305 agent threads across half a dozen projects. I make the architecture decisions, review the code and decide what ships. For material I plan to publish, I check agent output against primary sources. See my AI usage in numbers.

Now

November 2026.

  • Building MapSmith, with public examples for weather and earthquake data.
  • Working on my second commissioned geospatial application.
  • Maintaining Oriv as an open-source browser for Mac and iPhone.
